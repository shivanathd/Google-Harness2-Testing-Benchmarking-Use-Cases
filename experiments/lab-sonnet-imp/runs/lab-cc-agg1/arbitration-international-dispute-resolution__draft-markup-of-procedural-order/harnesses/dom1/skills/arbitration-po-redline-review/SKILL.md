# Draft procedural order: redline + objections letter

## 1. Operation
COMPARE the draft against (a) the controlling prior orders, (b) the ICC Rules / lex arbitri / evidence rules the orders adopt, (c) our client's strategy memo. Then RECONCILE: the memo is internal and is itself unverified; check every figure, paragraph number and date it gives against the primary documents before reusing it. Output per defect: accept / reject / revise, with replacement text and the instrument that supports it.

Keep distinct, and label as such: source text (quote it), matter fact, legal inference, risk, recommendation.

## 2. Build the baseline first (before drafting)
1. Paragraph map of the draft: list every paragraph number as printed. Drafts are often misnumbered (gaps, 14.x inside section II, 17.x/18.x/22.x/26.x/25.x out of order). Cite the number PRINTED in the draft; do not "fix" it silently; request renumbering in the letter.
2. Per prior order: what it fixed (dates, venue, hearing length, expert format reserved, confidentiality exceptions, modification/consultation clauses, waiver clause, costs reservation). A draft clause that overrides these is the strongest ground; quote the prior-order paragraph.
3. Date arithmetic: recompute every "N days before hearing" date, weekday and clock time (e.g. 48 hours before a 09:30 start is 09:30, not noon). Recompute hearing hours from the draft's own daily schedule (start/end minus breaks), then subtract opening/closing statements and the chess-clock total to see what remains for everything else. Compare with the memo's figure.
4. Check the draft's recitals against the prior timetable: it may record as filed what is not yet due, or give a different date for a ruling than the order that scheduled it.
5. Check internal consistency: cost-bearing clauses that contradict each other (interpretation, venue, indemnity vs "reasonable costs"), deadlines that cannot be sequenced (topic list vs joint expert statements), attendance rules that exclude persons the format needs.
6. Check what the draft OMITS that practice expects: costs submissions after the hearing, reservation of the privilege ruling, per-witness time reallocation, who bears interpreter cost.

## 3. Authorities
Cite only what is in the record or what you flag. Record-supported = cited in the prior orders or supplied rule texts; list them from the record before drafting. Anything else is from memory: use it only with an explicit "to be verified against the text before filing" note in the cover note to the supervising lawyer, not in the Tribunal-facing text as if confirmed. Check which edition of the rules (e.g. IBA Rules, ICC Rules) a prior order's article numbers belong to; flag mismatches, do not rely.
Keep seat, venue (hearing city), lex arbitri, governing law of the contract and rules separate. A hearing held elsewhere does not move the seat; the venue objection rests on the prior order's consultation requirement, not on jurisdiction. A purported waiver of set-aside/enforcement rights must be tested against the lex arbitri and party domicile.

## 4. Redline artifact contract
- Baseline = the draft's full text, every paragraph, in order. Changes appear as deletions of draft text and insertions of replacement text. Each objection carries, adjacent to the paragraph: ground (prior-order paragraph / rule), effect, accept/reject/revise, replacement language, and any fallback.
- The review tool for the deliverable may flatten the file: text extraction of a produced docx can show zero tracked-change markers and no margin comments even when the docx holds them; deleted text vanishes and comment grounds become invisible. So make the markup survive plain-text conversion: show deletions as strikethrough text and insertions as bold-underlined text in the body, put the annotation as a visibly labelled paragraph directly under the clause ("OUR OBJECTION n: ..."), and open the document with a short index table (draft paragraph / action / one-line ground). Real tracked changes/comments may be added in addition, but never as the only carrier. After building, re-read the file with the workspace reader and confirm that deleted text, inserted text and every annotation appear in the output.
- Untouched paragraphs stay verbatim (rejecting all changes should reproduce the draft). Preserve numbering and headings.
- Every revise item has actual replacement wording, not "should be reconsidered".

## 5. Cover letter contract
Addressed to the Tribunal through the channel the prior orders require, copied to opposing counsel and the Secretariat per the draft's communications clause (also any case-manager routing the prior orders require); signature block for the partner; placeholders only for date/signature, never for substance. Contents: framing sentence (comments invited by the stated deadline, redline enclosed, redline controls on detail); principal objections ranked by prejudice, each = draft paragraph + conflict with a prior order/rule + requested change; due-process framing (reasonable opportunity to present the case; equal treatment) rather than witness preference; reservation of rights.
Tribunal-facing text must NOT contain: the memo's reasoning, "devastating"/"bad faith" characterisations, internal minimum-hours tables, privileged communications with our own expert (do not enclose an internal expert email; an expert statement only if obtained and cleared). Fallback positions are disclosed only where the memo authorises a fallback; list them in the cover note to the partner as a choice. Describe third-party evidence only as the source document states it (what is confirmed vs inferred; the report's contents are unknown to us).
Separately, in the final chat reply (not in the filings): list memo errors found, record discrepancies, unverified citations, and decisions for the partner.

## 6. Defect checklist (method; test each against the draft, do not assume present)
- Memo vs record: re-verify every paragraph number, hours figure, weekday, venue/expert location and quoted statement the memo gives; list each error found. Where the memo asserts a negative (e.g. no consultation, no explanation), check what the draft itself recites and phrase it as "the draft records no ..." unless the record proves it.
- Draft vs prior orders: hearing length and start time, venue/seat language, expert format reserved to a later order or PHC after consultation, PHC timing, document-request phases and bars versus the timetable, confidentiality exceptions (insurers, auditors, legal advice, enforcement/challenge), modification-after-hearing clauses, waiver standard, costs reservation. Quote the prior-order paragraph for each.
- Draft internal defects: clauses that extend a rule set beyond the scope in which it was adopted; time-allocation rules that charge Tribunal questions or re-direct to a party or declare adjustments unreviewable; cost clauses that contradict each other (basis of assessment, interpreter cost, own-costs vs indemnity, application costs); deadlines that fall on the same day or cannot be sequenced; bars on applications; appointment of tribunal experts without consultation; waivers of set-aside/enforcement rights tested against the lex arbitri and party domicile; double-edged clauses that also bind our side (flag to the partner as a deliberate choice); missing costs-submissions step; misspelt party names or other caption errors.
- Third-party or expert material in the record: check sender domain versus signature block and letter addresses versus the address in the prior orders; flag before any statement is attached; do not overstate what an expert accepts or contests; separate what the source confirms from what the memo infers.
- Time arithmetic: if the system clock and the matter timetable differ in year, treat the matter deadline as live and state the assumption.
