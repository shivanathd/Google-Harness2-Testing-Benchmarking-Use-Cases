# Respondent's markup of a proposed interim order

Operation: COMPARE the proposed order (text under review) with (i) the application it implements (relief actually sought), (ii) the
tribunal's own standard in the procedural order, (iii) the arbitration agreement (limits on tribunal power), then TEST the client's
memo against all three, then MARK UP. Keep five layers apart in every note: what the draft says / what the source says (pinpoint) /
legal consequence / what we ask (complete replacement wording) / fallback. Keep seat, lex arbitri, contract law, institutional rules
and the arbitration clause distinct (tribunal power comes from the rules + lex arbitri + the clause; the clause can limit it).

## 1. Before drafting (scratch file, not output/)
- Source hierarchy: assignment -> partner's memo instructions -> procedural order (what the tribunal said) -> arbitration agreement
  (limits on power) -> tribunal/secretariat email -> application -> proposed order. Record each conflict and the version chosen.
- Audience: the markup is filed with the Tribunal and served on the Claimant (email invites a "marked-up version" with the response).
  It is EXTERNAL. Argue from the record and rules only; never carry the memo's privileged content (priorities, "strongest argument",
  internal assessments, evidence NIS does not yet have). Memo-authorised fallbacks (e.g. a lower cap, a narrowed carve-out) may appear
  in the note where the memo tells you to; anything else goes only in the final reply.
- Convention conflict: the memo asks for "margin comments"; the assessing reader shows body text only. Use tracked changes plus an
  adjacent inline bracketed note (redline-docx skill). Say so in the final reply.
- Table every order paragraph (incl. recitals, sub-paragraphs, trailing un-numbered paragraphs, signature/addresses): verdict
  accept / revise / delete / insert, source pinpoint, replacement text. Do not leave a consequential paragraph unaddressed. Where you accept
  a paragraph (e.g. 5-18 housekeeping, the seat/language recitals, three-member tribunal, English law), add a short "no change" note
  if the memo told you to concede it.
- Recompute numbers yourself: damages (54,000 MT x 879.63), shortfall percentages, uplift (65.0 - 47.5 = 17.5m, 36.8%), EBITDA
  decline, net-assets multiple; check each against the memo.
- Memo audit: tick each memo statement against the record; where wrong, follow the record in the markup and list each error once in
  the final reply (do not copy it, do not silently correct it).

## 2. Defect checklist (test each against the text; pinpoints below were verified in the workspace record)
Draft internal / recital accuracy
- Findings (order para 4): findings of breach, loss, and dissipation risk are merits/pre-judgment; the Answer (PO1 para 3) denies breach and
  pleads force majeure; the Application itself calls the shortfalls "beyond dispute" (App. para 96) and NIS "has not disputed" them,
  which PO1 para 3 contradicts. Replace with provisional prima facie language limited to this Order, volumes recorded as alleged,
  force majeure reserved. The order never applies the four PO1 para 15 elements (prima facie case; urgency; irreparable harm not
  reparable by damages; balance of convenience/proportionality) - require a finding on each, per measure.
- Recital accuracy against the record (each is a place the draft departs from the record - correct or bracket):
  Answer "raised counterclaims" (PO1 para 3 records only denial + force majeure; timetable says "Counterclaim, if any");
  witness-statement and expert-report dates (order: 21/20 April 2025; Application exhibit list: 18/17 April 2025);
  PetroChem article (order: 14 March 2025, "comprehensive restructuring ... separation of upstream and downstream"; Application paras 34, 55: 2 April 2025, "exploring options ... ring-fencing of downstream operations");
  recital 2(f) (order: decided on written submissions unless a hearing is requested; Secretariat email: Tribunal "reserves the right to convene an oral hearing" and Claimant may reply);
  defined term SIAA vs "IAA" in PO1; counsel addresses/emails (see section 4).
- Cap and measure do not match: para 5 caps the freeze at the "Frozen Amount", but para 7 (no financing, no dividends, no transfers at any value,
  no Grupo Andino sale/LOI) is uncapped and para 13 reverses the burden; para 6(g)/6(b) reach non-parties' accounts ("any entity controlled");
  para 5 purports to bind banks/third parties; para 15 binds non-parties; para 8 reaches third-party providers' documents.
- Cost clause (para 16) is self-contradictory (reserved to final award / Respondent may bear costs if measures "upheld" / each bears own).
- Duration (para 14): "until ... any period for challenge or annulment has expired" outlasts the award; Singapore seat -> set-aside, not "annulment".
- Para 11: adverse inferences on "any issue" for non-compliance plus a pre-agreed bar on opposing enforcement on jurisdiction/scope grounds is a
  waiver of jurisdictional and enforcement defences; delete the waiver.
- Para 12: contempt, imprisonment, USD 50,000/day payable to Claimant, strike-out of defences: penal; tribunal has no contempt power;
  strike-out forecloses force majeure. Offer only proportionate consequences (costs; adverse inference confined to the specific non-compliance).
- Para 17 vs confidentiality: PO1 para 16(a)-(d) and SOA 14.5 give narrower exceptions than order para 17(a)-(c) (bank/custodian disclosure,
  securities-law disclosure); disclosure to third parties needs Tribunal authorisation under PO1 para 16 - align or add an express authorisation limited to what is necessary.
- Para 9: litigation hold copy and 7/14-day timetable; hold notice may disclose privileged categories; propose a certification by an officer instead of copying the notice.
Source-specific
- PO1 para 15 gives the Tribunal a DISCRETION ("may") to require security/cross-undertaking, and puts on the party seeking modification the
  burden of showing changed circumstances. The memo calls cross-undertakings "mandatory/standard"; word the ask as a PO1 para 15 condition
  of any measure, and do not assert a duty the PO1 does not impose. Seek an express right to apply to vary/discharge and a periodic review.
- Uplift: App. paras 58-60 DO justify the extra USD 17.5m (interest, costs, expenses); the memo's "no justification" is wrong. The better point:
  PO1 para 2 records the USD 47.5m as already "inclusive of ... interest", so the interest limb double counts; costs are a separate, undetermined head.
- Worldwide scope: App. paras 62-63 concede NIS operates principally in Colombia; no asset-specific dissipation evidence is cited beyond EBITDA,
  the minority stake sale (USD 120m, announced 10 March 2025) and the press article. Memo-only facts (USD 3.2bn assets, USD 2.31bn net assets,
  ~4,200 staff, USD 1.6bn revenue, sale negotiated since June 2024) are NOT in the record: put them in notes only as "NIS will evidence" with
  a bracketed exhibit placeholder, or omit; flag in the final reply.
- Force majeure: PO1 para 3 cites SOA "Section 9"; the SOA excerpt places force majeure in Section 8 (Sections 9-13 omitted from the excerpt). App. para 97(b) says
  Section 8 requires notice "within 48 hours"; SOA 8.2 says ten (10) Business Days. Use Section 8 / 8.2 and flag the PO1 slip.
- Anti-suit (paras 10-11): SOA 14.4 bars any tribunal measure that would enjoin a Party from participating in proceedings before "any court or
  regulatory authority of the Party's home jurisdiction" (home jurisdiction = jurisdiction of incorporation, SOA 1.20: Colombia for NIS).
  Quote 14.4 verbatim. THE PIVOTAL WEAKNESS: the Application (para 36) describes the Bogotá forum as the "Tribunal de Arbitraje of the Bogotá
  Chamber of Commerce" - an arbitral body, arguably neither a "court" nor a "regulatory authority". The Application never mentions 14.4 and rests
  on 14.2/14.3 and Art 28(1). Build the note so it works either way: (i) 14.4 as written; (ii) the subject of the Bogotá action is the effect of a
  Colombian ministry resolution (regulatory/public-law), and para 10(b)-(c) and para 8(e) also reach "regulatory bod[ies]" and every
  court in Colombia, Singapore and the UK, so the order would block NIS's dealings with its own regulator and any set-aside/enforcement
  response before the seat court (also contrary to SOA 14.3 and PO1 para 15 final paragraph preserving court access); (iii) an interim injunction on a
  merits-overlapping forum is not necessary or proportionate: offer the fallback the memo authorises (carve out Colombian proceedings/authorities;
  limit to proceedings that seek merits determination of the SOA dispute). Do not call 14.4 conclusive; do not assume the Bogotá forum is a court;
  put "confirm the nature and status of the Bogotá forum" in the final reply.
- Authority: the memo forbids challenging the Tribunal's general power under ICC Rules Art 28(1) and IAA s.12(1)(i) (cited in PO1 para 15). Do not.
  Any further statutory subsection (e.g. order para 11's "Section 12(6)") or ICC article not reproduced in the record goes in a
  "verify before filing" list; never quote its text from memory.
Missing safeguards to insert (full operative wording, consistent numbering and cross-references)
- Ordinary-course carve-out (payroll, trade creditors, tax, operating expenditure, existing contracts incl. performance of the SOA, insurance and regulatory compliance, and compliance with Colombian law);
  cross-undertaking/security under PO1 para 15; review (e.g. every 90 days) and an express liberty to apply; a stated standard (four PO1 para 15 elements);
  a clause that nothing in the Order is a finding on the merits; a sunset (e.g. 180 days unless renewed) as the memo's alternative; notification threshold
  and scope as the memo directs (USD 10m, disposals/encumbrances of fixed assets or equity interests only, not routine operations).
- Preservation: narrow to SOA documents, the Q3-Q4 2024 deliveries, the force majeure events and relevant production records; delete "all other ULSD counterparties"
  (or confine to volumes allocated away from KEH in Q3-Q4 2024, on a showing of relevance); temporal scope per the memo; reciprocal obligation on the Claimant to preserve (flag as a choice).

## 3. Build and verify
1. Load `redline-docx`; one tracked edit + one inline note per change. Every note: paragraph number, what the draft says, the source pinpoint
   (SOA / PO1 / Application para / Secretariat email), why it matters, the replacement, the fallback if the memo authorises it. Numbering of inserted paragraphs (e.g. 7A, 14A) must not
   disturb existing cross-references; update any cross-reference your changes strand (para 3, 8(a)-(f), 9, 10, 11).
2. The accepted view must read as a complete, internally consistent order: re-read it end to end for stale figures (65,000,000, 100,000, 7 days, 14 days, 50,000), stale defined terms,
   and mismatches between recitals and operative text.
3. No internal/privileged vocabulary in the file (memo, privileged, strategy, "strongest", "fallback" as a label of our own thinking): grep it.
4. Today's date is not a record date: use the record timeline (Secretariat email; response due 13 May 2025; memo draft due 7 May 2025) for the author date; do not flag the deadline as missed.

## 4. Final reply (short, for the supervising lawyer)
Delivered file and conventions used (inline notes rather than comment balloons; not rendered visually); positions taken; memo errors found (uplift "no justification", cross-undertaking "mandatory", PO1 prima facie point is correct); record anomalies
(counsel address/email conflicts across PO1, the Application, the Secretariat email and the order - the PO1 addresses are the Tribunal's own record, none is confirmed as correct, so bracket them; Section 8/9; exhibit dates; the memo signature block carries NIS's registered address, not the firm's);
the Bogotá-forum risk; memo facts needing evidence; items to verify before filing; client decisions not made (admitting delivery volumes, the Grupo Andino sale, a voluntary Bogotá undertaking).
