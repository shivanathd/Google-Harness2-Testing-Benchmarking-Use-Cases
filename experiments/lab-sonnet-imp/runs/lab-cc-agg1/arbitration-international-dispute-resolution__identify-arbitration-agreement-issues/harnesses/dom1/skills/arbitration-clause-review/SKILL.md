# Arbitration clause review across an instrument suite

Operation: COMPARE each dispute clause against the others, RECONCILE them with the counterparty's description of them, CLASSIFY by severity, DRAFT replacement language. Audience: the supervising lawyer at the client's outside counsel, who will mark up the documents.

## 1. Build the record before writing
- Extract, per instrument: arbitration agreement (scope words, "existence/validity/termination"), institution + rules edition, seat vs hearing venue, number/appointment of arbitrators and clock start, governing law of contract vs of arbitration, language, discovery/evidence, appeal/finality, interim relief, costs, confidentiality, limitation, damages limits, joinder/consolidation, notices, precedence ("conflict") clause, exhibits that the dispute standard depends on. Put these in a snapshot table (rows = terms, columns = instruments) and cite section numbers.
- Pathological-clause tests for each clause: Is the named institution/rule set real and does it cover this use (e.g. an "expert determination" under an institution's rules - confirm those rules exist as named; if you cannot verify, say so)? Is any step "final and binding" yet challengeable? Is any threshold undefined (how measured, aggregation)? Does any clock lack a start event? Can the clause bind everyone who will be in the dispute (non-signatory affiliates, co-manufacturers)?
- Trace the precedence clauses of ALL instruments together; check whether they point at each other (circularity) and which dispute clause each says wins.

## 2. Separate five things in every issue
Source text (quote it, with section) / matter fact (email, memo) / legal inference / risk / recommendation. Do not let the counterparty's summary stand in for clause text: for each statement in a summary memo or client email about a clause, check it against the clause and list every divergence (overstated carve-outs, wrong model law, mischaracterised scope, "mutual" where text names no party, wrong "all claims" scope, factual claims such as distances or precedents). Note also that an anecdote offered as rationale may not actually be answered by the clause it is used to justify (e.g. an enforcement-resistance anecdote used to justify an appeal right: check what the anecdote actually turned on).

## 3. Client positions drive ranking
Read the client emails for stated priorities, walk-away positions and exposure figures; map each priority to an issue ID and say whether the record shows it as instruction or only as preference. Severity scale must be defined in the memo; rank by recoverability/enforceability/forum-fragmentation risk, and state the client's own ranking where it differs. Every client-priority item must have its own entry or an explicit "not an issue because...". Enforceability of the award where the client will enforce (identify the enforcement jurisdiction from the client emails, and the treaty/statute route it implies) is a distinct item: seat's courts, award vs non-award (expert determinations are not necessarily awards), appeal/set-aside routes, assets location, and whether each instrument's clause yields an enforceable award. Do not omit it because other items are louder.

## 4. Authorities
Cite only what you can verify from the record or an authorised source. Institutional-rule article numbers, statute sections and case law stated from memory must be labelled "unverified - confirm with local counsel" and collected in a list of points to confirm (jurisdiction, proposition, issue ID). For joinder/consolidation/multiple-contract points, identify the actual rule provision in the edition applicable and its conditions (same or compatible arbitration agreements, consent) rather than asserting that consolidation is available. Never assert a foreign-law outcome as settled when unchecked.

## 5. Recommendations
For each issue: accept/reject/revise stance, precise replacement wording for the core fixes (not just "harmonise"), a fallback, and the negotiating realism (what the counterparty's stated preferences imply). Give one coherent target architecture (single dispute article covering all instruments, one precedence rule, one governing law/seat or a stated allocation, consolidation/joinder consent, targeted document production, aligned limitation) and check each recommendation against the others for contradictions and against any client walk-away position.

## 6. Status and audience checks
- Check dates against today's date: if signing/effective dates in the record have passed, state whether the documents are drafts or executed and give both pre-signing and amendment framing; do not assume either silently.
- Internal client emails and the client's positions are privileged/internal: mark in the memo that they must not be quoted to the counterparty, and write markup suggestions so they do not reveal walk-away positions.
- Before finishing, confirm: every instrument has a row in the snapshot; every precedence clause is traced; every statement in the counterparty memo is tested; every client priority is mapped; every issue has provisions + severity + effect + recommendation; every unverified authority is in the confirm-list; the file reopens and the memo reads as one consistent document (IDs, cross-references and counts match).
