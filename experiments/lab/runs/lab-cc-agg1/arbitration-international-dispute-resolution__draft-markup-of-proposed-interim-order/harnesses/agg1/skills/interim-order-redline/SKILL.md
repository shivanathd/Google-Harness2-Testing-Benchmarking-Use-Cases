# Interim order redline (arbitration)

Use when the assignment is to mark up, comment on or object to a proposed interim order. The deliverable is the redlined order. The supervising lawyer must be able to file or circulate it without repair. The file goes to the Tribunal, so anything that is not for the Tribunal goes in the final message.

## 1. Sources and what each controls

- The proposed order is the baseline. Reject-all must reproduce it exactly.
- The arbitration agreement, as the contract recites it: clause, seat, number of arbitrators, language, governing law, and any limit on the tribunal's injunctive power. Quote the words of any limit you rely on, with its section.
- The procedural order(s). They control the matter terms: seat, rules, the interim-measures standard, security or cross-undertaking, modification, privilege, confidentiality, representation block, communications and timetable. Cite the paragraph.
- Rule and statute text. Only text in the file counts (section 7).
- The application and its exhibits: the other side's pleaded case, dates and figures. Use them to test the draft's recitals. Read the whole application, using the reading rule in the system prompt. A paragraph you cite must have been read.
- The client's strategy memo gives instructions. Its quotations, figures and paragraph references are hypotheses until checked against the instrument they cite.

## 2. Measure-by-measure test

For each operative paragraph, record: the measure; the test element the controlling order states for it; the record the applicant relies on; and the point the respondent contests. Leave the decision to the tribunal. Do not state an outcome as settled law.

## 3. Findings and orders must agree

- Every operative paragraph must rest on a finding that the redline leaves standing. If the redline removes or negates the finding a measure depends on, strike or narrow the measure, or name the conflict as a decision for the lawyer. Do not leave a measure standing on a finding the same redline contradicts. Check each "the Tribunal hereby orders" against the findings above it.
- After deletions, check numbering, lettering gaps, "[Reserved]" placeholders and every cross-reference to a deleted paragraph.

## 4. Concessions in replacement wording

- Before replacement text finds, accepts or "notes" a fact, check the respondent's pleaded position. A provisional finding that the applicant has established a prima facie case on a fact the respondent disputes is a concession, even when labelled provisional.
- Where the client's instruction prescribes wording that concedes such a point, put in the file the least concessive wording that still does what the instruction asks, for example a recital that records the applicant's allegation and the respondent's denial and leaves the element to the tribunal. In the final message, give the instruction's own wording and name the concession it makes, so the lawyer can choose. A concession that is filed is hard to withdraw; a file without one is easy to amend.
- A replacement finding of law (for example, that a money claim cannot show irreparable harm) is a legal proposition. Treat it as unverified unless the file supports it.

## 5. Forum and scope limits

- For each forum a restraint would reach, record its name, the type the record describes it as, and its jurisdiction. Test each limb of the order against the words of any contractual limit: a limit in specific words reaches what those words reach. Do not infer a forum's type from its name. If the record does not settle the type, bracket it for local counsel.
- Check whether a restraint on the other side's forum engages the arbitration clause the respondent itself relies on. The applicant may plead that the filing breaches that clause.
- Geographic reach, duration and review: list each jurisdiction a measure reaches and tie it to the record. Check the modification mechanism in the procedural order. Compare any notification or reporting threshold with the entity's figures in the record.
- Recompute amounts from the claim's own inputs (volume times price, schedules), not from a memo's figures.

## 6. Cross-references and identifiers

- Check every section, paragraph, date, name and address cited in the draft against the source it cites. Where two sources conflict, bracket the draft text and name the conflict. Do not choose silently.
- The procedural order's representation block is the controlling version unless the record shows it is wrong. Bracket a party's own address where its sources disagree.

## 7. Rule and authority discipline

- Do not state what an article or statute says, or omits, unless its text is in the file. An application or order that quotes an article is a source for the quotation only.
- In the filed text, use at most a short bracket such as "[local counsel to confirm]". The reasons, and the list of rule points relied on but not verified, go in the final message.

## 8. Privilege and internal notes

- The Tribunal-facing file carries no internal analysis, strategy-memo content or internal-only notes. Put those in the final message. Do not add them as comments for someone to delete later.
- Where a privilege point arises, state it neutrally in the comment on the paragraph it affects, applying the narrowest test the procedural order states.

## 9. Before finishing

- Reject-all text equals the source order. Accept-all text reads as one coherent order: no lettering gaps, no references to deleted paragraphs, and no finding that contradicts a measure it supports.
- Count the comments and tracked revisions. Each changed paragraph has its comment. Comments do not appear in a plain-text read of the file, so the final message must carry the grounds for each change.
- Read the produced file back and check the accepted text against the instruction.
- Compare the system date with the response deadline in the record. If the deadline has passed, say so and draft to the record timetable.
- Final message, in this order, each item kept short:
  (a) decisions for the supervising lawyer: concessions, with the instruction's wording; conflicts left open; measures kept or removed and why;
  (b) blockers and deadlines;
  (c) source conflicts left unresolved;
  (d) rule text relied on and not verified;
  (e) what could not be checked;
  (f) one line per changed paragraph: paragraph number, change, controlling source.

## 10. Matter notes from the first record this skill was used on (verify before reuse)

- PO1 para. 15 states four elements: a prima facie case on the merits, urgency, irreparable harm not adequately reparable by damages, and balance of convenience with proportionality. It makes a cross-undertaking discretionary and allows modification on changed circumstances. The strategy memo cites ICC Article 28(1) for these points; the file's source for them is PO1 para. 15.
- SOA s.14.4 bars the tribunal from enjoining a party from participating in proceedings "before any court or regulatory authority of the Party's home jurisdiction". The application describes the Colombian proceeding as before a "Tribunal de Arbitraje" of the Bogotá Chamber of Commerce, and pleads that its filing breaches s.14.2 (application para. 74). The draft's anti-suit paragraph reaches Singapore and the UK, which s.14.4 does not reach on its words.
- PO1 para. 3 attributes the force majeure defence to "Section 9" of the SOA. Force majeure is Section 8, which the application also cites.
- Counsel addresses differ across PO1 para. 10, the draft, the application and the memo. The memo's signature block gives the client's registered office, not the firm's address.
- Quantum: 54,000 MT (31,200 MT in Q3 plus 22,800 MT in Q4) times USD 879.63 is USD 47,500,020.
- Check the witness statement, expert report and press-report dates against the application's list of materials before use.
