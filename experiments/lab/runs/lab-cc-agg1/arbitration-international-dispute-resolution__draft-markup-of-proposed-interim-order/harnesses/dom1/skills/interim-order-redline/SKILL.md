# Interim order redline (arbitration)

Use when the assignment is to mark up, comment on or object to a proposed interim order. The deliverable is the redlined order. The supervising lawyer must be able to file or circulate it without repair.

## 1. Sources and what each controls

- The proposed order is the baseline. Reject-all must reproduce it exactly.
- The arbitration agreement, as the contract recites it: the clause, seat, number of arbitrators, language, governing law, and any limit on the tribunal's power to order interim measures. Cite the section and quote the words of any limit you rely on, taken from the instrument in the file.
- The procedural order(s). They control the matter terms: seat, rules, the interim-measures standard, security or cross-undertaking, modification, privilege, confidentiality, representation block, communications routing and timetable. Cite the paragraph, and read it for the elements it states.
- Rule and statute text (institutional rules, lex arbitri). Only what is in the file counts. See section 7.
- The application and its exhibits: the applicant's pleaded case, dates and figures. Use them to test the draft's recitals.
- Client instructions (strategy memo). They are instructions, not evidence. Its quotations, figures, dates, paragraph references and assertions about what a party did or did not provide are hypotheses until checked against the document they describe.
- Tribunal communications: the response deadline, the format and the filing route.

## 2. Measure-by-measure test

For each operative paragraph, record: the measure; the elements the controlling procedural order states for it (take them from that order, not from memory); the record the applicant relies on; and the point the respondent contests. Leave the tribunal's decision to the tribunal. Do not state an outcome as settled law.

Where the draft recites a standard, check that it is the standard the procedural order states, and that the draft's findings address each element the order requires.

## 3. Consistency between findings and orders

- Every operative paragraph must rest on a finding that the redline leaves standing. After you replace any finding, re-read every measure that depends on it. If the replacement negates an element a measure depends on, strike or narrow that measure, or name the conflict as a decision for the lawyer. A measure kept with a "limited" scope on a finding that the same redline denies is still a conflict.
- After deletions, check numbering, lettering gaps, "[Reserved]" placeholders, and every cross-reference to a deleted or moved paragraph, including cross-references in the recitals.
- Read the accepted text for phrases that two edits have duplicated, for example the same qualifying phrase added to a paragraph and again to its first sub-paragraph.

## 4. Concessions in replacement wording

- Before replacement text finds, accepts or "notes" a fact, check the respondent's pleaded position. A provisional finding that the applicant has established a test element on a fact the respondent disputes is a concession, even when labelled provisional. Where the client's instruction supplies such wording, do not adopt it silently. Offer the least concessive wording the instruction allows, and name the concession in the comment and in the final message as a decision for the lawyer.
- A replacement finding of law is a legal proposition. Treat it as unverified unless the file supports it.
- Before a comment says that the other side gave no reasons, figures or evidence for a point, search the application, its witness statements and its expert reports for that point. A client memo's assertion of this kind is a hypothesis. If the application gives a reason, say what it gives and why it is still open to challenge, as the record shows.

## 5. Forum and scope limits

- For each forum or authority a restraint would reach, record its name as the file gives it, its type as the file describes it (court, regulatory authority, arbitral body, other) and its jurisdiction. Do not infer the type from the name. If the file does not establish the type, bracket the point for local counsel.
- Test each limb of the order against the words of any contractual limit. A limit in specific words reaches what those words reach and no more. Check each jurisdiction each limb names against that limit.
- Check whether a restraint on the other side's forum engages the arbitration clause the respondent itself relies on, and whether the applicant pleads that the filing breaches that clause.
- Geographic reach, duration and review: list each jurisdiction a measure reaches and tie it to the record. Check the modification mechanism in the procedural order. Compare any notification or reporting threshold with the entity's figures in the record.
- Recompute amounts from the claim's own inputs (volume times price, schedules, rounding the applicant states), not from a memo's totals.

## 6. Cross-references, identifiers and dates

- Check every section, paragraph, date, party name and address cited in the draft, the procedural order, the instrument and the application against the source it cites. Where two sources conflict, bracket the draft text and name the conflict in the comment. Do not choose silently.
- Where the memo, the application and the draft cite different section numbers for the same provision, use the instrument's own numbering and name the discrepancy.
- Counsel names, addresses and email domains: the procedural order's representation block is the controlling version unless the record shows it is wrong. Bracket a party's own address where its sources disagree.
- Dates in recitals: check each against the document it describes, including the date of any witness statement or report and the edition date of any publication cited.

## 7. Rule and authority discipline

- Do not state what an article or statute says, or omits, unless its text is in the file. A record that cites an article gives its number, not its text. Where the point matters, mark it "[rule text to be verified]" in the comment and list it in the final message.
- Where a client memo attributes a power to a different rule source from the one the procedural order uses, cite the procedural order's paragraph and name the discrepancy.
- Do not write a conclusion about what an article does or does not provide (for example, whether it provides for security) into a comment or the final message unless the text is in the file.

## 8. Privilege and the filed file

- The Tribunal-facing file carries no internal analysis, strategy-memo content, memo references, or internal-only notes. Do not add such notes as comments in the file for someone to delete before filing. A file that needs comments removed before it can be filed is not usable without repair. Put internal points in the final message.
- Where a privilege point arises, state it neutrally in the comment on the paragraph it affects, applying the narrowest privilege the controlling procedural order states, with its paragraph cited.
- Comments cite the instrument, the procedural order's paragraph and the record. They do not cite the client memo.

## 9. Before finishing

- Reject-all text equals the source order. Accept-all text reads as one coherent order: no lettering gaps, no references to deleted paragraphs, no finding that contradicts a measure it supports, no duplicated phrases.
- Count the comments and tracked revisions. Each changed paragraph has its comment. Count comments that carry an internal or privileged tag: the count in the filed file must be zero.
- Read the produced file back and check the accepted text against the instruction.
- Compare the system date with the response deadline in the tribunal's communication. If the deadline has passed, say so, and draft to the record timetable.
- A plain-text read of a .docx may return only the accepted text: comments and deleted text may not appear. The grounds for each change must therefore also appear in the final message.
- Final message, in this order:
  (a) decisions for the supervising lawyer: concessions, conflicts left open, measures kept or removed and why, and provisions added beyond the client's instructions;
  (b) blockers and deadlines;
  (c) source conflicts left unresolved;
  (d) rule text relied on and not verified;
  (e) what could not be checked;
  (f) one line per changed paragraph: paragraph number, change, controlling source.
