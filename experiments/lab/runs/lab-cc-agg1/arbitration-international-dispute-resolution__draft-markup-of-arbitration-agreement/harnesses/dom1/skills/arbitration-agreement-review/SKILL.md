# Arbitration agreement review and redline

## Work product and audience

- **Redline**: the counterparty's text with Word tracked changes, not silent replacement. Each changed provision carries a bracketed comment that names the playbook section, its classification (Required, Preferred, or Fallback), and the fallback position or "None" where the playbook states one. Drafting additions that are not playbook terms are labelled as additions.
- **Internal drafts** carry a privilege header. Comments that quote a privileged playbook or disclose fallbacks must never reach the counterparty's version. Say so in the memo.
- **Cover memo** to the supervising lawyer: bottom line; issues grouped in the matter's own priority tiers; for each issue, the counterparty form, the markup, and the fallback or escalation; open items and decisions; drafting additions; timing; negotiation notes.

## Keep these distinct

- **Arbitration agreement** (the operative clause) versus **underlying transaction agreement** (its governing law, notice clause, defined terms such as capital call or drag-along). The underlying agreement is a separate instrument. If it is not in the file, every cross-reference and every playbook requirement that extends to it is unverified and goes in the open items.
- **Seat** (legal place; supervisory court for vacatur and enforcement), **hearing location**, **governing law of the arbitration agreement**, and **procedural rules**. A playbook requirement applies to one of these. Changing another does not satisfy it.
- **Administering institution** and rules version. Emergency-measures and appellate rules are separate optional rule sets; name them where the playbook requires them.

## Checks for every review

1. **Institution trigger**: test the playbook's own trigger for the required institution (for example, domestic versus cross-border) against the facts on the record: party domicile, target domicile, asset location.
2. **Tribunal**: number of arbitrators, threshold amount, appointment deadlines, default appointing authority, chair selection. Test each threshold against the amount in the record.
3. **Defined-term sweep after any change to tribunal structure**: every use of arbitrator, arbitrators, Tribunal, and Arbitrator must work for the structure the agreement now adopts. A reference that applies only to a sole arbitrator must say so. Qualifications must bind every arbitrator, including each member of a panel.
4. **Court relief**: a carve-out in operative text; a statement that seeking relief is not a waiver of arbitration and not a submission to the court beyond the relief sought; any exclusive-remedy clause cross-refers to the carve-out; emergency arbitrator rules incorporated by name.
5. **Class, collective, and representative waiver**: absence is itself a departure, not silence.
6. **Consolidation and joinder**: consent requirements. Tribunal "sole discretion" is a departure wherever the playbook requires consent.
7. **Discovery, hearings, evidence**: numerical caps stated in the agreement where the playbook requires express terms. "Rules without modification" is not a cap.
8. **Confidentiality**: every category the playbook lists, only the exceptions the playbook allows, and the remedy it requires.
9. **Remedies and damages**: check the remedies clause for language that reinstates a damages category a waiver removes (for example, "not limited in the types or amounts of damages"). Confirm the waiver covers the remedies, the legal theories, and the carve-outs the playbook requires.
10. **Costs**: fee-shifting terms; "each party bears its own" is a departure. Administrative and arbitrator fee allocation in the same article must match the fee clause.
11. **Limitations**: contractual period against the statutory period under the governing law; the playbook floor; the accrual or discovery trigger.
12. **Award**: timetable, the defined trigger date, and required content (for example, findings of fact and conclusions of law). Resolve every bracketed phrase in operative text.
13. **Finality and appeal**: the finality clause cross-refers to any appeal mechanism. List any collision between appeal windows and expedited deadlines as a decision for the client.
14. **Document handling**: any destruction obligation is a departure where the playbook prohibits it. Check the retention period and the playbook's fallback.
15. **Cross-references**: every Article and Section referred to exists in the final numbering. Re-check after all insertions and deletions.

## Decision rules

- **Position status** comes from the playbook's body label for that section. **Negotiation tier** comes from the playbook's priority summary. Where they disagree, mark up to the body label and list every section where they disagree, not only the first ones noticed. Ask the client to confirm.
- **Required positions** are not conceded in the draft. Escalate as the playbook's escalation protocol directs. Where no fallback exists, write "None".
- **Derived figures**: recompute any figure the document derives from others (contribution divided by stake percentage gives an implied total value; component amounts sum to totals). Flag non-reconciling figures. Change them only when instructed.
- **Dates**: collect every deadline from the instructions and the counterparty correspondence, including forwarded and direct internal emails from the supervising lawyer and the client. Where they conflict, list each with its sender and date, and work to the earliest.
- **Addresses**: check the sender and recipient addresses and firm names against the signature blocks before any outward send.

## Before finishing

- Each departure from a Required position has a comment with the section number, classification, and fallback or "None".
- Each absent required provision is inserted as operative text, not only commented on.
- The defined-term sweep and cross-reference check (checks 3 and 15) have been run on the final text.
- No comment gives a section two different tiers.
- Memo rows cite the section numbers of the final redline.
- Open items include every label mismatch, every unverified cross-reference, every derived-figure mismatch, and every deadline conflict.
