# Arbitration clause issues review

Use for a review memo on dispute-resolution terms. Typical output: a .docx memo with a severity table, one entry per issue (problem, effect, recommendation), an accuracy check of any third-party or client analysis, and open items.

## 1. Keep these layers separate

For each provision, say which layer it operates on. Do not let one layer stand in for another.

- The arbitration agreement itself: which disputes it covers ("arising out of or relating to" is wider than narrower wording).
- The law governing the contract on the merits. Instruments in one transaction often choose different laws.
- The law governing the arbitration agreement. Instruments often do not choose it expressly.
- The curial law of the seat, which governs court supervision, set-aside, and any appeal or challenge route.
- The institutional rules and the version they name. Check the rules text against the record or a source you can cite.
- Tribunal authority: what the tribunal may award or order. A damages bar in the arbitration clause limits the tribunal; the same bar in the commercial terms limits liability. Where both exist, analyse both.
- The procedural timetable: appointment windows, notice periods, commencement, tolling.
- Enforcement posture: the route by which an award or decision becomes enforceable where the client expects to collect.

## 2. Per-instrument anatomy

For each instrument, locate and record each item below, or record it as absent:

seat and hearing venue (separately); institution and rules; number of arbitrators, how they are chosen, and any election deadline; language; governing law; consolidation or joinder; interim relief and court carve-outs; document production; costs and fee-shifting; confidentiality and its carve-outs; finality, appeal or recourse language; the commencement trigger and limitation period; precedence or conflict clauses; notice mechanics for commencing a claim; exhibits, schedules and annexes, including any marked "to be attached".

Exhibits, schedules and annexes are part of the instrument. Record each one as attached, placeholder, or missing. A placeholder that a dispute depends on is itself a finding.

## 3. Cross-instrument reconciliation

- Build the comparison as a table across instruments, one row per item in section 2. Each row where the instruments differ is either a conflict to resolve or a deliberate split to justify. Say which.
- A pair of precedence clauses that each name the other is one circular issue. State which way each points.
- Where two clauses could each claim the same dispute, that is a forum issue even if no party has raised it.
- Third-party memos, client emails and instruction notes are positions and analysis, not operative text. For every factual claim they make about the instruments (section numbers, amounts, dates, "mutual" or "one-way" terms, what a clause says), check the instrument and list each mismatch in an accuracy section.
- Where the client has ranked its priorities, show where your severity rating agrees with that ranking and where it departs, with the reason.

## 4. Enforcement posture

- For an award to be enforced in a U.S. court, identify the route: the New York Convention as implemented in Chapter 2 of the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. §§ 201 to 208). Confirm against the statute text before relying on it.
- An expert determination or other non-arbitral decision is not an award under the Convention. Say how it would be enforced (usually as a contract term), that the route is uncertain, and mark that point for confirmation.
- Any appeal, set-aside or challenge route in the seat's courts is a delay risk for enforcement. State the mechanism and its effect on the enforcement timetable.
- Where the client has asked whether the seat, or a specific enforcement court, is acceptable, answer the question in the memo, with reasons drawn from the instruments and the legal framework. Do not leave it unanswered.

## 5. Recommendations

- Quote the words of each clause you propose to delete or change, and confirm those words appear in the cited section before you recommend the change. Recommending removal of an exception that the clause does not contain is an error.
- Where the assignment asks for revised language, give precise replacement text. Otherwise state what the replacement must achieve.
- Severity: Critical = no workable forum, or caps recovery on the most likely loss; High = material cost, delay or enforcement risk, fixable by targeted drafting; Medium = drafting defect with bounded impact; Low = clean-up or consistency item.
- Privilege: where a source includes a non-lawyer on its distribution, state the privilege risk in one paragraph and leave the conclusion to counsel.

## 6. Before finishing

- Coverage: every instrument's seat and governing law appears in the memo body with its section. If the summary says there are N governing laws or M seats, each one is named in the body.
- Questions: every question the client's correspondence puts to counsel has an answer in the memo or appears under open items.
- Full-text claim: do not describe the review as covering the full text of an instrument unless its exhibits, schedules and signature page were read. A read that stops before the document does (for example, mid-table at an exhibit heading, with no closing section) is not full coverage: read the rest by offset or from the saved output first. If any part was not read, say which.
- Counts: apply the counts step of the `deliverable-check` skill to the memo and the final message.
- Legal propositions not in the record: mark them [Verify] in the memo and list each in open items with who should confirm it.
- Final message: list the departures from the client's positions, the unverified legal points, and the placeholders found in the instruments.
