# Procedural order redline and objections

Use when a draft procedural order has been circulated for party comments. Typical outputs: a tracked-changes redline of the draft with comments, and a cover letter to the Tribunal summarising objections and relief.

## 1. Sources and what each one does

- The draft as circulated is the baseline. The redline's reject-all view must reproduce it.
- Prior procedural orders control the matter terms: seat, rules, hearing dates and length, timetable, venue, document production framework, communications routing, costs. Cite the prior-order paragraph whenever you say the draft departs from it.
- Keep these distinct and cite each to its own source: the arbitration clause (as the orders recite it); the governing law of the contract; the lex arbitri, which governs challenge and set-aside; the procedural rules (ICC Rules; IBA Rules only as guidelines, where a prior order says so); and the Tribunal's procedural power as the draft invokes it.
- Strategy memos, client correspondence and expert emails are instructions and analysis. Their figures, dates and paragraph references are hypotheses until checked against the instrument.

## 2. Issue checks

Run these on every changed or contested paragraph.

- Arithmetic: recompute hours, per-witness time and totals from the draft's own schedule and allocation, not from a memo's assumed daily hours. Remove opening and closing time before comparing with the cross-examination allocation.
- Dates: convert each "N days before the hearing" or "hours before the first day" into a calendar date and time, check the weekday, and count hours exactly.
- Cross-references: check that each paragraph number cited in the draft, the memo or the letter exists and says what the citing text says. Check the draft's own numbering for gaps and out-of-order sections, and note them where your comments depend on the numbers.
- Repeated dates across orders (conference dates, ruling dates, deadlines): reconcile them and flag any mismatch. Do not silently pick one.
- Names, caption and addresses: conform them to the record.
- Unilateral change: where the draft alters a term a prior order set, check whether that order or the rules text requires notice or consultation, and cite the paragraph that does.

## 3. Rule and authority discipline

- Cite a rule or statute only from the matter record or a source you can check. A record that cites an article number does not contain the rule's text. If you state what an article requires and its text is not in the file, mark that point as unverified in your final message. Do not put the marking inside the Tribunal-facing letter.
- Where a point turns on the parties' domicile or seat, for example whether a set-aside waiver is available, state the facts from the record that decide it, and mark the legal conclusion for local-counsel confirmation.
- Do not state facts about correspondence, earlier agreement or party positions that the record does not contain. Bracket the sentence, for example "[Respondent to confirm: ...]", and list it in the final message. A Tribunal must not receive an unverified statement that a party did or did not agree to something.

## 4. Third-party and expert material

- Before relying on a letter or email in filed text, compare its sender domain and signature address with the addresses for the same party and firm in the procedural orders. A mismatch is a reason to hold reliance and flag it, not to rely on the material silently.
- Characterise an expert's report only from the report. If the report is not in the file, say so; an email summary is not the report.
- If the strategy file makes a signed statement or written confirmation a precondition to submission, the Tribunal-facing text must not present an unsigned view as the expert's position. Bracket it or leave it out, and list it as pending.
- Disclosing new material (a document, a third-party letter, a privileged fact) to the Tribunal and opposing counsel is a client decision. Name it as one in the final message. Do not describe the package as ready to file while such a decision is open.

## 5. Deliverable anatomy

Redline (.docx):
- Tracked insertions and deletions against the draft. Reject-all reproduces the draft text; accept-all reads as a clean order. Keep headings, numbering and caption except where you change them, and make any change to the caption a tracked change too.
- One comment per changed or contested paragraph, authored by the firm, giving the objection, the controlling prior-order paragraph, and the replacement language.
- Comments and tracked deletions may not survive a plain-text read of the .docx. Before relying on the text conversion, check how it renders your tracked changes and comments on a test copy. The objection and the replacement must therefore also appear in the cover letter.

Cover letter (.docx):
- Address the Arbitrator through the ICC Secretariat, as the prior order's communications provision requires, and copy counsel for the other party and the Secretariat as the draft requires. Do not use the Arbitrator's direct contact details.
- Structure: a short opening (what is enclosed and the comment deadline); objections grouped by subject, each with the draft paragraph numbers and the controlling prior-order paragraph; relief sought as a numbered list matching the redline; reservation of rights; signature placeholders; enclosure; copy line.
- Every paragraph changed in the redline must have its ground stated in the letter.
- No internal analysis, privileged strategy or unresolved client decision goes into the letter.
- Leave the date and signatures as placeholders where they are unconfirmed.

## 6. Before finishing

- Compare the redline's changed paragraph numbers with the grounds in the letter. Each changed paragraph must be covered.
- Confirm that each figure and date in the letter traces to the draft or a prior order. Bracket any that do not.
- Check that the redline's reject-all and accept-all views match the description above, and that the letter names every changed paragraph.
- Final message, in this order: (a) decisions for the supervising lawyer (disclosure of new material, reliance on unverified third-party material, bracketed facts); (b) any blocker to filing; (c) source inconsistencies found and left unresolved; (d) rule text relied on but not verified; (e) what could not be checked.
- If the system date is after the draft's comment deadline, draft to the record timetable and say the filing may be moot.
